Read the following passage and answer the questions below:

"Banknotes have a natural advantage over coins in that they are lighter to carry but are also less durable. The durability of coins means that even if metal coins melt in a fire or are submerged under the sea for hundreds of years they still have some value when they are recovered. Gold coins salvaged from shipwrecks retain almost all of their original appearance, but silver coins slowly corrode. The different disadvantages between coins and banknotes imply that there may be an ongoing role for both forms of bearer money, each being used where its advantages outweigh its disadvantages. Most banknotes are made from cotton paper. Over the years, a number of materials other than paper have been used to print banknotes. This includes various textiles, including silk and materials such as leather. Silk and other fibers have been commonly used in the manufacture of various banknote papers, intended to provide both additional durability and security. Polymer banknotes were developed to improve durability and prevent counterfeiting through incorporated security features such as optically variable devices that are extremely difficult to reproduce."

Questions:
a) What are the advantages and disadvantages of banknotes over coins?
b) How gold coins are more durable than silver ones?
c) Which materials are used for making banknotes?
d) What are the advantages of polymer banknotes?
e) What is the precis of the passage?
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Answers to Comprehension Questions:

  1. Advantages and disadvantages of banknotes over coins:
    The primary advantage of banknotes over coins is that they are significantly lighter and more convenient to carry in large values. Their main disadvantage is that they are much less durable than metallic coins, being prone to tearing, fire, and water damage.
  2. Durability of gold coins over silver coins:
    Gold coins are chemically inert and do not tarnish; even when submerged under the sea in shipwrecks for centuries, they retain almost all of their original lustrous appearance. In contrast, silver coins slowly react with surrounding substances and corrode over time.
  3. Materials used for making banknotes:
    Historically and currently, banknotes have been produced from high-grade cotton rag paper, textiles such as silk, materials like leather, and modern synthetic polymer substrates reinforced with embedded security fibers.
  4. Advantages of polymer banknotes:
    Polymer banknotes offer far superior physical durability (lasting 2.5 to 4 times longer than paper notes), resist moisture and dirt, and incorporate sophisticated anti-counterfeiting features such as optically variable devices (OVDs) and transparent windows that are exceedingly difficult to duplicate.
  5. Precis of the passage:
    Title: Co-existence of Coins and Banknotes
    While banknotes are light and easy to handle, coins possess unmatched physical longevity. Cotton paper, silk fibers, and modern synthetic polymers have evolved to make paper currency more resilient and counterfeit-resistant, ensuring both forms of bearer currency serve complementary monetary functions.

Short Notes:

a) Social Effects of Rohingya Refugees on Bangladesh:
Hosting over 1.2 million forcibly displaced Rohingya refugees from Myanmar has imposed immense socioeconomic strain on Bangladesh, particularly in Cox's Bazar and Bhasan Char. The sudden influx altered local demographics, led to massive deforestation (over 6,000 acres of reserve forests cleared), depleted groundwater reserves, and depressed local unskilled wage rates. Social tensions between the host community and refugees, rise in cross-border drug and arms trafficking (Yaba trade), and security concerns in camp areas represent ongoing socioeconomic challenges.

b) Operation Searchlight:
Operation Searchlight was the brutal, premeditated military crackdown launched by the Pakistan Army on the night of 25 March 1971 to crush the Bengali nationalist movement and independence aspirations. Initiated under the supervision of General Tikka Khan, the operation unleashed indiscriminate genocide against unarmed civilians, intellectuals, political leaders, and students in Dhaka (Dhaka University, EPR headquarters at Pilkhana, Rajarbagh Police Lines) and across East Pakistan, sparking the historic declaration of Bangladesh's independence and the Liberation War.

c) CAMELS Rating in Banking:
CAMELS is an international standardized supervisory rating system used by bank regulatory authorities (including Bangladesh Bank) to evaluate the overall financial health and operational soundness of commercial banks. The acronym stands for:

  • C – Capital Adequacy (measured by Risk-Weighted Capital Ratio)
  • A – Asset Quality (proportion of Non-Performing Loans and provisioning)
  • M – Management Capability (corporate governance and compliance)
  • E – Earnings Quality (profitability, ROA, and ROE)
  • L – Liquidity (ability to meet short-term obligations, CRR, and SLR)
  • S – Sensitivity to Market Risk (exposure to interest rate and foreign exchange fluctuations)

d) Structure and Responsibilities of Union Parishad:
Union Parishad (UP) is the lowest administrative tier of rural local self-government in Bangladesh, constituted under the Local Government (Union Parishad) Act, 2009.

  • Structure: Comprises 13 elected members: 1 Chairman, 9 General Ward Members, and 3 Female Members elected from reserved seats (1 for every 3 wards).
  • Responsibilities: Maintenance of rural public law and order (village police/Chowkidar), maintenance of local infrastructure (culverts, rural roads), birth and death registration, dispute resolution via Village Courts (Gram Adalat), distribution of government social safety net allowances (VGF, VGD, old-age pensions), and rural sanitation.

e) Abdulrazak Gurnah:
Abdulrazak Gurnah, a Tanzanian-born British novelist, was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2021 "for his uncompromising and compassionate penetration of the effects of colonialism and the fate of the refugee in the gulf between cultures and continents." His acclaimed novels include Paradise (1994), By the Sea (2001), and Afterlives (2020), which portray the psychological exile and displacement of post-colonial migrants.

f) Brexit:
'Brexit' (a portmanteau of 'British' and 'exit') refers to the formal withdrawal of the United Kingdom from the European Union (EU). Following a nationwide referendum on 23 June 2016 (51.9% voting to leave), the UK invoked Article 50 of the Lisbon Treaty. After intensive negotiations, the UK formally exited the EU on 31 January 2020, ending nearly 47 years of membership and concluding the EU–UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement to govern trade, security, and immigration.

Note Characteristics & Security Features:

a) Available Banknotes in Bangladesh & Issuing Authority:
In Bangladesh, currency notes are divided into two distinct legal categories:

  1. Government Notes (সরকারি নোট): Tk. 2 and Tk. 5 notes (and coins of Tk. 1, 2, 5). These are issued directly by the Ministry of Finance, Government of the People's Republic of Bangladesh, and bear the signature of the Senior Finance Secretary.
  2. Banknotes (ব্যাংক নোট): Tk. 10, Tk. 20, Tk. 50, Tk. 100, Tk. 200, Tk. 500, and Tk. 1,000 notes. Under the Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972, Bangladesh Bank holds the sole monopoly right to design, issue, and manage banknotes, which bear the signature of the Governor of Bangladesh Bank.

b) Intaglio Line (ইনট্যাগ্লিও লাইন / খোদাইকৃত প্রিন্ট):
What it is: Intaglio printing is an advanced security printing process where ink is deposited from recessed grooves on engraved metal plates under immense pressure, leaving a raised, tactile layer of thick ink on the paper surface.
Where and Why it is used:

  • Where: Applied on high-denomination banknotes (Tk. 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000) on the portrait of Bangabandhu, the denomination figures, Bangladesh Bank lettering, and specific slanted tactile lines along the borders.
  • Why: First, it provides a distinctive raised feel that cannot be replicated by commercial color photocopiers or digital printers. Second, slanted intaglio lines serve as tactile indicators to allow blind and visually impaired individuals to identify the note denomination by touch (e.g., 7 slanted lines for Tk. 1000, 5 for Tk. 500, 3 for Tk. 100).

c) Five Key Security Features of Tk. 1,000 Denomination Note:

  1. High-Resolution Watermark: Features a clear portrait of Father of the Nation Bangabandhu Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, with the bright electrotype numerical figure '1000' and Bangladesh Bank's monogram located directly beneath.
  2. Color-Shifting Optically Variable Ink (OVI): The numeral '1000' printed on the top-right corner shifts distinctly from golden-yellow to green when tilted.
  3. Holographic Windowed Security Thread: A 4mm wide security thread containing the Bangladesh Bank logo and the micro-text '1000 টাকা' that shifts colors and glows under UV light.
  4. Tactile Intaglio Printing: Raised tactile lines on the right border (seven slanted lines) for the visually impaired, and raised printing on the portrait, 'এক হাজার টাকা', and Bangladesh Bank lettering.
  5. Microprinting and Latent Image: Microprinted repeating text "BANGLADESH BANK" embedded along lines invisible to the naked eye, and a latent image of '1000' appearing inside the bottom border when viewed horizontally at eye level.

Solve the following Mathematical Problems (Describe and illustrate whenever necessary):

a) Algebra: If \( a = 3 \), then find the value of \( a^6 + \frac{1}{a^6} \).

b) Equations: 10 cows can graze in a field for 15 days and 20 cows can graze in the same field for 10 days. For how many days can 30 cows graze in the field?

c) Average: Three math classes X, Y and Z take an algebra test. The average scores in class X, Y and Z are 83, 76 and 85 respectively. The average score of all students on classes X and Y together is 79. The average score of all students on classes Y and Z together is 81. What is the average for all the three classes?

d) Interest: In how many years, Tk. 150 will produce the same interest at 8%, Tk. 800 produce in 3 years at \( 4\frac{1}{2}\% \)?

e) Mensuration: A rectangular plot has a concrete path running in the middle of the plot parallel to the breadth of the plot. The rest of the plot is used as a lawn, which has an area of \( 240 \text{ m}^2 \). If the width of the path is 3m and the length of the plot is greater than its breadth by 2m, what is the area of the rectangular plot?
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Mathematical Solutions:

a) Algebra:

Given \( a = 3 \).
\[ a^6 = 3^6 = 729 \]
\[ a^6 + \frac{1}{a^6} = 729 + \frac{1}{729} = \frac{729 \times 729 + 1}{729} = \frac{531441 + 1}{729} = \frac{531442}{729} \approx 729.00137 \]
Answer: \( \frac{531442}{729} \) (or 729.0014).

b) Equations (Pasture Problem):

Let the initial amount of grass in the field be \( g \) units.
Let the growth rate of grass per day be \( r \) units.
Let 1 cow consume \( c \) units of grass per day.
Condition 1: 10 cows graze for 15 days:
Total grass eaten = \( g + 15r = 10 \times 15 \times c = 150c \quad \text{--- (i)} \)
Condition 2: 20 cows graze for 10 days:
Total grass eaten = \( g + 10r = 20 \times 10 \times c = 200c \quad \text{--- (ii)} \)
Subtracting (i) from (ii):
\[ (g + 10r) - (g + 15r) = 200c - 150c \implies -5r = 50c \implies r = -10c \]
(Alternatively, treating grass diminishing):
From (ii) minus (i): \( 5r = 50c \implies r = 10c \) (if order reversed), then \( g = 100c \).
Let 30 cows graze for \( x \) days:
\[ g + xr = 30xc \implies 300c - 10xc = 30xc \implies 40xc = 300c \implies x = \frac{300}{40} = 7.5 \text{ days} \]
Answer: \( 7\frac{1}{2} \) days (7.5 days).

c) Average (Alligation of 3 classes):

Let the number of students in classes X, Y, and Z be \( P, Q, \) and \( R \) respectively.
Given averages: \( \bar{X} = 83, \bar{Y} = 76, \bar{Z} = 85 \).
Combined average of X and Y = 79:
\[ \frac{83P + 76Q}{P + Q} = 79 \implies 83P + 76Q = 79P + 79Q \implies 4P = 3Q \implies P = \frac{3}{4} Q \]
Combined average of Y and Z = 81:
\[ \frac{76Q + 85R}{Q + R} = 81 \implies 76Q + 85R = 81Q + 81R \implies 4R = 5Q \implies R = \frac{5}{4} Q \]
Now, overall average for all three classes \( \bar{A} \):
\[ \bar{A} = \frac{83P + 76Q + 85R}{P + Q + R} \]
Substituting \( P = \frac{3}{4}Q \) and \( R = \frac{5}{4}Q \):
\[ \text{Numerator} = 83\left(\frac{3}{4}Q\right) + 76Q + 85\left(\frac{5}{4}Q\right) = \frac{Q}{4} [249 + 304 + 425] = \frac{978}{4} Q = 244.5 Q \]
\[ \text{Denominator} = \frac{3}{4}Q + Q + \frac{5}{4}Q = \frac{12}{4} Q = 3Q \]
\[ \bar{A} = \frac{244.5 Q}{3 Q} = 81.5 \]
Answer: 81.5.

d) Simple Interest:

Interest from Tk. 800 in 3 years at \( 4\frac{1}{2}\% = 4.5\% \):
\[ I = \frac{P \times r \times t}{100} = \frac{800 \times 4.5 \times 3}{100} = 8 \times 13.5 = 108 \text{ Tk.} \]
Now for Tk. 150 at 8% to produce Tk. 108 interest in \( n \) years:
\[ 108 = \frac{150 \times 8 \times n}{100} = \frac{1200n}{100} = 12n \]
\[ n = \frac{108}{12} = 9 \text{ years.} \]
Answer: 9 years.

e) Mensuration:

Let the breadth of the rectangular plot be \( x \) meters.
Then the length of the plot is \( (x + 2) \) meters.
Total area of the plot = \( x(x + 2) \) \( \text{m}^2 \).
The concrete path runs across the middle parallel to breadth, with width = 3m.
Area of the path = \( 3 \times x = 3x \) \( \text{m}^2 \).
Area of the lawn = Total area - Path area = \( 240 \text{ m}^2 \):
\[ x(x + 2) - 3x = 240 \]
\[ x^2 + 2x - 3x = 240 \implies x^2 - x - 240 = 0 \]
Factoring:
\[ (x - 16)(x + 15) = 0 \]
Since breadth cannot be negative, \( x = 16 \) meters.
Breadth = 16 m, Length = \( 16 + 2 = 18 \) m.
Total Area of the rectangular plot = \( 16 \times 18 = 288 \text{ m}^2 \).
Answer: 288 \( \text{m}^2 \).

Focus Writing: Climate Diplomacy

Introduction: Climate change has emerged as one of the most pressing existential challenges of the twenty-first century. Rising global temperatures, melting polar ice caps, sea-level rise, and the escalating frequency of extreme weather disasters threaten global food security, human habitats, and geopolitical stability. In this critical juncture, Climate Diplomacy has evolved from a niche environmental dialogue into a paramount instrument of foreign policy and multilateral diplomacy, bridging scientific realities with international geopolitical agreements.

Understanding Climate Diplomacy: Climate diplomacy refers to the diplomatic negotiations, strategic foreign policy maneuvers, and bilateral/multilateral collaborations aimed at addressing global climate change. It operates on the recognition that anthropogenic climate change is a transboundary crisis that cannot be resolved by any single sovereign nation acting in isolation.

Key Stakeholders and Multilateral Architecture:

  1. United Nations and UNFCCC: The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) serves as the primary global forum, organizing the annual Conference of the Parties (COP) where landmark treaties like the Kyoto Protocol (1997) and the Paris Agreement (2015) were negotiated.
  2. National Governments: Sovereign states negotiate national commitments (Nationally Determined Contributions - NDCs) to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, balance economic growth, and allocate climate budgets.
  3. Non-Governmental Organizations (NGOs) & Civil Society: Environmental advocacy groups exert immense moral and scientific pressure, holding governments and multi-national corporations accountable.
  4. Private Sector & Financial Institutions: Clean-tech corporations, green bond issuers, and development banks mobilize climate finance toward renewable energy and sustainable agriculture.

Core Challenges in Climate Negotiations:

  • North-South Geopolitical Divide: Historical emissions originate largely from industrialized nations, while climate-vulnerable countries (such as Bangladesh and Pacific Island nations) bear the catastrophic brunt. The principle of Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR) remains a fierce battleground.
  • Climate Finance Commitments: Developed nations have persistently fallen short of fulfilling their pledge to mobilize $100 billion annually to assist developing countries in climate adaptation and mitigation.
  • Loss and Damage Fund: Operationalizing the Loss and Damage Fund established at COP27 remains hindered by bureaucratic friction regarding funding sources and accessibility.

Way Forward and Conclusion: Global climate diplomacy must shift from symbolic rhetoric to binding, accountable action. Decarbonizing the global energy grid, sharing green technologies, strengthening climate adaptation finance, and prioritizing vulnerable populations are imperative. Ultimately, climate diplomacy is not merely about preserving ecosystems; it is about defending human civilization, international peace, and intergenerational justice.
