প্রশ্ন নং ১ Part-A: Analytical Ability Part-A: Analytical Ability পূর্ণমান: ৫
A train travels 300 km at a constant speed. If the speed had been 10km/h faster, the train would have taken 1 hour less to cover the same distance. What is the original speed of the train?
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Solution:

Let the original speed of the train be \( x \) km/h.
Distance \( d = 300 \) km.
Time taken at original speed \( t_1 = \frac{300}{x} \) hours.
When speed is increased by 10 km/h, new speed = \( (x + 10) \) km/h.
New time taken \( t_2 = \frac{300}{x + 10} \) hours.
According to the problem:
\[ t_1 - t_2 = 1 \implies \frac{300}{x} - \frac{300}{x + 10} = 1 \]
\[ 300 \left( \frac{x + 10 - x}{x(x + 10)} \right) = 1 \]
\[ 300 \times 10 = x(x + 10) \implies x^2 + 10x - 3000 = 0 \]
Factoring the quadratic equation:
\[ x^2 + 60x - 50x - 3000 = 0 \]
\[ x(x + 60) - 50(x + 60) = 0 \]
\[ (x - 50)(x + 60) = 0 \]
Since speed cannot be negative, \( x = 50 \) km/h.
Answer: 50 km/h.

প্রশ্ন নং ২ Part-A: Analytical Ability Part-A: Analytical Ability পূর্ণমান: ৫
A shopkeeper mixes two types of rice, one costing Tk. 40/kg and the other Tk. 60/kg, to create a mixture worth Tk. 52/kg. If the total weight of the mixture is 50kg, determine the quantity of each type of rice used in the mixture.
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Solution:

Let the quantity of rice costing Tk. 40/kg be \( x \) kg.
Then the quantity of rice costing Tk. 60/kg is \( (50 - x) \) kg.
According to the question, total value of the mixture:
\[ 40x + 60(50 - x) = 52 \times 50 \]
\[ 40x + 3000 - 60x = 2600 \]
\[ -20x = 2600 - 3000 = -400 \]
\[ x = \frac{400}{20} = 20 \text{ kg} \]
Quantity of Tk. 60/kg rice = \( 50 - 20 = 30 \) kg.
Alternatively, by Alligation:
Ratio of Tk. 40 to Tk. 60 = \( (60 - 52) : (52 - 40) = 8 : 12 = 2 : 3 \).
Quantity of Tk. 40 rice = \( 50 \times \frac{2}{5} = 20 \) kg.
Quantity of Tk. 60 rice = \( 50 \times \frac{3}{5} = 30 \) kg.
Answer: 20 kg of Tk. 40/kg rice and 30 kg of Tk. 60/kg rice.

প্রশ্ন নং ৩ Part-A: Analytical Ability Part-A: Analytical Ability পূর্ণমান: ৫
If \( x^2 - 3x + 1 = 0 \), find the value of \( \left(x^2 - \frac{1}{x^2}\right) \).
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Solution:

Given: \( x^2 - 3x + 1 = 0 \)
Dividing both sides by \( x \):
\[ x - 3 + \frac{1}{x} = 0 \implies x + \frac{1}{x} = 3 \quad \text{--- (i)} \]
Now we find \( \left(x - \frac{1}{x}\right) \):
\[ \left(x - \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 = \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right)^2 - 4 = 3^2 - 4 = 9 - 4 = 5 \]
\[ \implies x - \frac{1}{x} = \sqrt{5} \quad \text{--- (ii)} \]
Now evaluate \( x^2 - \frac{1}{x^2} \):
\[ x^2 - \frac{1}{x^2} = \left(x + \frac{1}{x}\right) \left(x - \frac{1}{x}\right) = 3 \times \sqrt{5} = 3\sqrt{5} \]
Answer: \( 3\sqrt{5} \).

প্রশ্ন নং ৪ Part-A: Analytical Ability Part-A: Analytical Ability পূর্ণমান: ৫
In a row of boys, if A who is 10th from the left and B who is 9th from the right interchange their positions, A becomes 15th from the left. How many boys are there in the row?
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Solution:

Originally:
A is 10th from the left.
B is 9th from the right.
When A and B interchange positions, A occupies B's former position.
Now, this new position of A (which is B's original position) is:
- 15th from the left.
- 9th from the right (since it was B's original position).
Total number of boys in the row = (Position from left) + (Position from right) - 1:
\[ \text{Total} = 15 + 9 - 1 = 23 \]
Answer: 23 boys.

প্রশ্ন নং ৫ Part-A: Analytical Ability Part-A: Analytical Ability পূর্ণমান: ৫
A train traveled at an average speed of 100 km/hr, stopping for 3 minutes after every 75km. How long did it take to reach its destination 600 km from the starting point?
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Solution:

Total running distance = 600 km.
Running speed = 100 km/hr.
Running time without stoppage = \( \frac{600}{100} = 6 \) hours.
Now determine number of stoppages:
The train stops after every 75 km.
Total 75 km intervals = \( \frac{600}{75} = 8 \).
However, the 8th stoppage corresponds to the arrival at the final destination, where the journey ends.
Therefore, the number of stoppages during the journey = \( 8 - 1 = 7 \).
Total stoppage time = \( 7 \times 3 \text{ minutes} = 21 \text{ minutes} \).
Total time taken = 6 hours + 21 minutes = 6 hours 21 minutes.
Answer: 6 hours 21 minutes.

প্রশ্ন নং ৬ Part-B: Accounting Part-B: Accounting পূর্ণমান: ৬
Ott Company had 210,000 ordinary shares outstanding on December 31, 2023. During the year 2024, the company issued 8,000 shares on May 1 and retired 14,000 shares on October 31. For the year 2024, Ott Company reported net income of 229,690. Required: What earnings per share data should be reported at the bottom of its income statement?
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Solution:

Step 1: Calculate Weighted-Average Number of Shares Outstanding for 2024:

  • Beginning shares (Jan 1 – Dec 31): \( 210,000 \times \frac{12}{12} = 210,000 \) shares
  • Shares issued on May 1 (held for 8 months): \( 8,000 \times \frac{8}{12} = +5,333.33 \) shares
  • Shares retired on October 31 (retired for 2 months): \( 14,000 \times \frac{2}{12} = -2,333.33 \) shares

Weighted-average ordinary shares = \( 210,000 + 5,333.33 - 2,333.33 = 213,000 \) shares.

(Alternatively, month-by-month):
Jan 1 – April 30 (4 months): \( 210,000 \times \frac{4}{12} = 70,000 \)
May 1 – Oct 31 (6 months): \( (210,000 + 8,000) \times \frac{6}{12} = 218,000 \times \frac{6}{12} = 109,000 \)
Nov 1 – Dec 31 (2 months): \( (218,000 - 14,000) \times \frac{2}{12} = 204,000 \times \frac{2}{12} = 34,000 \)
Total Weighted-Average Shares = \( 70,000 + 109,000 + 34,000 = 213,000 \) shares.

Step 2: Calculate Basic Earnings Per Share (EPS):
\[ \text{EPS} = \frac{\text{Net Income} - \text{Preferred Dividends}}{\text{Weighted-Average Ordinary Shares}} \]
Since preferred dividends = 0:
\[ \text{EPS} = \frac{229,690}{213,000} \approx 1.078 \text{ or Tk. 1.08 (or 1.078 Tk.)} \]
Answer: Earnings Per Share (EPS) = Tk. 1.078 per share.

প্রশ্ন নং ৭ Part-B: Accounting Part-B: Accounting পূর্ণমান: ৬
MMZ Company's relevant information for last month is given below:
Sales (15,000 units × Tk. 30 per unit) = Tk. 450,000
Variable expenses (15,000 units) = Tk. 315,000
Fixed expenses = Tk. 90,000

Required:
a) What is the CM ratio?
b) Determine the break-even point in quantity and in amount.
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Solution:

Given Data:
Selling price per unit \( P = \text{Tk. } 30 \)
Variable cost per unit \( V = \frac{315,000}{15,000} = \text{Tk. } 21 \)
Contribution Margin per unit \( CM = P - V = 30 - 21 = \text{Tk. } 9 \)
Total Sales Revenue = Tk. 450,000
Total Variable Expenses = Tk. 315,000
Total Contribution Margin = \( 450,000 - 315,000 = \text{Tk. } 135,000 \)
Fixed Expenses = Tk. 90,000

a) Contribution Margin (CM) Ratio:
\[ \text{CM Ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Contribution Margin}}{\text{Total Sales Revenue}} \times 100\% = \frac{135,000}{450,000} \times 100\% = 30\% \]
(Or \( \frac{CM \text{ per unit}}{P} = \frac{9}{30} = 0.30 = 30\% \)).
Answer: 30%.

b) Break-Even Point (BEP):
1. Break-Even Point in Quantity (Units):
\[ \text{BEP (Units)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Expenses}}{\text{CM per unit}} = \frac{90,000}{9} = 10,000 \text{ units.} \]
2. Break-Even Point in Amount (Taka):
\[ \text{BEP (Amount)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Expenses}}{\text{CM Ratio}} = \frac{90,000}{0.30} = \text{Tk. } 300,000 \]
(Alternatively: \( 10,000 \text{ units} \times 30 = \text{Tk. } 300,000 \)).
Answer: 10,000 units and Tk. 300,000.

প্রশ্ন নং ৯ Part-C: Finance Part-C: Finance পূর্ণমান: ৬
The Alif Company's next expected dividend is Tk. 3.18; its growth rate is 6 percent; and its stock currently sells for Tk. 36. New stock can be sold to net the firm Tk. 32.40 per share.
a) What is Alif's percentage flotation cost?
b) What is Alif's cost of new common stock?
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Solution:

Given:
Next expected dividend \( D_1 = \text{Tk. } 3.18 \)
Constant growth rate \( g = 6\% = 0.06 \)
Current market price \( P_0 = \text{Tk. } 36 \)
Net proceeds per share \( P_{net} = \text{Tk. } 32.40 \)

a) Percentage Flotation Cost:
Flotation cost per share \( F = P_0 - P_{net} = 36 - 32.40 = \text{Tk. } 3.60 \).
\[ \text{Percentage Flotation Cost } (f) = \frac{P_0 - P_{net}}{P_0} \times 100\% = \frac{3.60}{36} \times 100\% = 10\% \]
Answer: 10%.

b) Cost of New Common Stock (\( K_e \)):
Using Gordon's Dividend Growth Model with flotation cost:
\[ K_e = \frac{D_1}{P_0(1 - f)} + g = \frac{D_1}{P_{net}} + g \]
\[ K_e = \frac{3.18}{32.40} + 0.06 = 0.09815 + 0.06 = 0.15815 = 15.815\% \text{ or } 15.81\% \]
Answer: 15.81%.

প্রশ্ন নং ১০ Part-C: Finance Part-C: Finance পূর্ণমান: ৬
Consider the following scenario analysis:
- Scenario: Recession, Probability = 0.20, Rate of Return Stocks = -5%, Rate of Return Bonds = +14%
- Scenario: Normal economy, Probability = 0.60, Rate of Return Stocks = +15%, Rate of Return Bonds = +8%
- Scenario: Boom, Probability = 0.20, Rate of Return Stocks = +25%, Rate of Return Bonds = +4%

Required: Calculate the expected rate of return and standard deviation for each investment. Which one should you choose?
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Solution:

1. Expected Rate of Return \( E(R) = \sum P_i R_i \):

For Stocks:
\[ E(R_S) = (0.20 \times -5\%) + (0.60 \times 15\%) + (0.20 \times 25\%) \]
\[ = -1.0\% + 9.0\% + 5.0\% = 13.0\% \]
(Book note: If recession return is 0.20 * (-5) = -1.0, 0.60 * 15 = 9.0, 0.20 * 25 = 5.0 => 13.0%)

For Bonds:
\[ E(R_B) = (0.20 \times 14\%) + (0.60 \times 8\%) + (0.20 \times 4\%) \]
\[ = 2.8\% + 4.8\% + 0.8\% = 8.4\% \]

2. Standard Deviation \( \sigma = \sqrt{\sum P_i [R_i - E(R)]^2} \):

For Stocks (\( E(R_S) = 13\% \)):
- Recession: \( (-5 - 13)^2 = (-18)^2 = 324 \times 0.20 = 64.8 \)
- Normal: \( (15 - 13)^2 = (2)^2 = 4 \times 0.60 = 2.4 \)
- Boom: \( (25 - 13)^2 = (12)^2 = 144 \times 0.20 = 28.8 \)
\( \sigma^2_S = 64.8 + 2.4 + 28.8 = 96.0 \)
\( \sigma_S = \sqrt{96.0} \approx 9.798\% \approx 9.80\% \).

For Bonds (\( E(R_B) = 8.4\% \)):
- Recession: \( (14 - 8.4)^2 = (5.6)^2 = 31.36 \times 0.20 = 6.272 \)
- Normal: \( (8 - 8.4)^2 = (-0.4)^2 = 0.16 \times 0.60 = 0.096 \)
- Boom: \( (4 - 8.4)^2 = (-4.4)^2 = 19.36 \times 0.20 = 3.872 \)
\( \sigma^2_B = 6.272 + 0.096 + 3.872 = 10.24 \)
\( \sigma_B = \sqrt{10.24} = 3.20\% \).

3. Investment Decision:
Coefficient of Variation (CV) = \( \frac{\sigma}{E(R)} \):
\( CV_{\text{Stocks}} = \frac{9.80}{13} \approx 0.754 \)
\( CV_{\text{Bonds}} = \frac{3.20}{8.4} \approx 0.381 \)
A risk-averse investor should choose Bonds due to significantly lower risk (lower standard deviation of 3.2% vs 9.8% and lower CV). A risk-seeking investor seeking maximum wealth creation may choose Stocks for its higher expected return of 13%.

What is a power system, and what are its main components?
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Power System & Its Components:

Definition: A power system (or electrical grid) is an interconnected network of electrical components designed to generate, transmit, and distribute electrical energy from source points to residential, commercial, and industrial consumers safely, reliably, and efficiently.

Key Stages and Main Components:

  1. Generation (Power Plants): The source where electrical power is generated from primary energy sources—fossil fuels (natural gas, coal, oil), nuclear energy, or renewable resources (hydro, solar, wind, biomass). Key apparatus: Turbines, Alternators/Generators.
  2. Transmission System: The high-voltage network that transports bulk electricity over long distances from generating stations to load-center grid substations. High voltages (e.g., 400 kV, 230 kV, 132 kV) are used to minimize transmission \( I^2R \) losses. Key apparatus: Step-up Transformers, High-voltage Transmission Lines (pylons/cables), Switchgear.
  3. Substations: Facilities where voltage levels are transformed using step-down power transformers, and power flow is regulated, switched, and protected using circuit breakers and relays.
  4. Distribution System: The low-voltage network that safely delivers electricity from distribution substations directly to end-user consumers (typically 33 kV, 11 kV down to 400V/230V). Key apparatus: Distribution Transformers, Feeder Lines, Energy Meters.
  5. Protection and Control: Protective relays, switchgears, SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition) systems, and EMS (Energy Management Systems) that monitor and safeguard grid frequency and stability.

Describe the advantages and disadvantages of quick rental power plants on Bangladesh's economy.

Economic Analysis of Quick Rental Power Plants in Bangladesh:

Introduction: Quick Rental Power Plants (QRPPs) were emergency, short-term barge-mounted or containerized power generation facilities introduced under the 'Quick Enhancement of Electricity and Energy Supply (Special Provisions) Act 2010' to alleviate acute power shortages.

Advantages (Positive Economic Impact):

  1. Rapid Crisis Mitigation: QRPPs were deployed within a matter of 3 to 9 months, averting immediate economic paralysis caused by chronic 8-10 hour load shedding.
  2. Sustaining Industrial Productivity: Reliable power supply saved Bangladesh's export engine—the Ready-Made Garments (RMG) sector—from missed international export deadlines, preserving millions of jobs.
  3. GDP Growth Continuity: Uninterrupted power supported continuous manufacturing, commercial activities, and SME operations, sustaining a 6%+ national GDP growth trajectory during the 2010-2018 decade.

Disadvantages (Severe Long-term Economic Drain):

  1. Staggering Capacity Charges: Contracts included mandatory 'Capacity Payments' ensuring plant owners received billions of Taka regardless of whether electricity was dispatched, draining over Tk. 1 lakh crore from public finances.
  2. High Production Cost & Subsidies: Heavily dependent on imported diesel and furnace oil (HFO), making unit generation costs exorbitant (Tk. 15–25/kWh compared to Tk. 3–5 for base-load gas/coal), triggering massive fiscal subsidies for the Power Development Board (BPDB).
  3. Foreign Exchange Reserve Drain: Enormous fuel import bills severely depleted Bangladesh's sovereign foreign exchange reserves during global energy price shocks.
  4. Inflationary Pressure: Massive accumulated sector deficits compelled the government to repeatedly hike retail power tariffs, fueling national cost-push inflation.

The Role of Renewable Energy in the Power Sector of Bangladesh

Renewable energy represents a transformative and indispensable pillar for the long-term energy security, fiscal sustainability, and climate resilience of Bangladesh's power sector. Historically, Bangladesh has maintained heavy dependence on fast-depleting domestic natural gas reserves and expensive imported fossil fuels (diesel, furnace oil, and coal), making the national energy matrix highly vulnerable to international price volatility and foreign currency constraints. In this context, clean energy—particularly utility-scale grid solar, rooftop solar net-metering, and onshore wind power—offers an economically viable and carbon-neutral alternative. Under the Mujib Climate Prosperity Plan (MCPP) and updated integrated energy master plans, Bangladesh has targeted achieving 30% renewable electricity by 2030 and 40% by 2041. Expanding solar-powered irrigation pumps has already begun replacing millions of diesel pumps in agriculture, substantially reducing rural fuel consumption. Furthermore, innovative solutions such as floating solar on vast water bodies and regional hydropower imports from Nepal and Bhutan can help overcome domestic land scarcity constraints. Modernizing the national transmission grid with smart grid technology, battery energy storage systems (BESS), and green financing will enable Bangladesh to transition from high-carbon fiscal deficits toward an environmentally sustainable and self-reliant power economy.
