Essay: Affordable and Clean Energy (SDG 7)

Introduction: Energy is the lifeblood of modern civilization. It powers our homes, fuels industries, sustains digital connectivity, and drives national economic progress. However, conventional reliance on fossil fuels—coal, oil, and gas—has generated severe environmental degradation, accelerated global climate change, and created energy insecurity. Consequently, the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 7 (SDG 7) emphasizes ensuring access to "Affordable, Reliable, Sustainable and Modern Energy for All" by 2030.

The Imperative for Clean and Affordable Energy:

  1. Environmental Protection: Transitioning to clean renewable energy sources—solar, wind, hydroelectric, and geothermal—is essential to curbing greenhouse gas emissions and meeting the Paris Agreement temperature targets. Unlike fossil fuels, renewable energy does not emit harmful particulate matter, substantially improving public health and air quality.
  2. Economic Growth and Employment: Clean energy technologies create millions of high-skilled jobs in solar installation, grid engineering, battery manufacturing, and system maintenance. Lowering long-term levelized costs of energy (LCOE) makes renewable electricity affordable for small enterprises and rural communities.
  3. Energy Security & Independence: Domestic renewable energy sources insulate developing economies like Bangladesh from volatile international oil and gas market shocks, conserving vital foreign exchange reserves.

Challenges & Steps Forward: Expanding clean energy requires substantial upfront capital, smart grid integration, efficient energy storage systems (BESS), and consistent regulatory frameworks. Governments must eliminate fossil-fuel subsidies, incentivize rooftop solar net-metering, and foster public-private partnerships. In conclusion, affordable and clean energy is not just an environmental aspiration; it is an economic prerequisite for creating an equitable, prosperous, and sustainable future for mankind.

প্রশ্ন নং ৩ গণিত গণিত পূর্ণমান: ৩৫
Solve the following problems:

a) Ratio and Proportion: A man spent \( \frac{1}{2} \) of his money and then lost \( \frac{1}{4} \) of the remainder. He was left with Tk. 3,600. How much did he start with?

b) Function: If \( f(x, y) = \frac{x^2 - 2xy + y^2}{x^2 - y^2} \), then \( f(-x, -y) = ? \)

c) Set: In a class of 120 students numbered 1 to 120, all even numbered students opt for Physics, those whose numbers are divisible by 5 opt for Chemistry and those whose numbers are divisible by 7 opt for Math. How many opt for none of the three subjects?
💡 সমাধান ও বিশ্লেষণ দেখতে ক্লিক করুন ▼

Mathematical Solutions:

a) Ratio and Proportion:

Let the man start with Tk. \( 8x \) (or \( M \)).
He spent \( \frac{1}{2} \) of his money = \( \frac{1}{2} \times 8x = 4x \).
Remainder = \( 8x - 4x = 4x \).
He lost \( \frac{1}{4} \) of the remainder = \( \frac{1}{4} \times 4x = x \).
Amount left = \( 4x - x = 3x \).
According to the question:
\[ 3x = 3,600 \implies x = 1,200 \]
Initial amount he started with = \( 8x = 8 \times 1,200 = \text{Tk. } 9,600 \).
Answer: Tk. 9,600.

b) Function:

Given function:
\[ f(x, y) = \frac{x^2 - 2xy + y^2}{x^2 - y^2} = \frac{(x - y)^2}{(x - y)(x + y)} = \frac{x - y}{x + y} \]
Now evaluate \( f(-x, -y) \):
\[ f(-x, -y) = \frac{(-x)^2 - 2(-x)(-y) + (-y)^2}{(-x)^2 - (-y)^2} = \frac{x^2 - 2xy + y^2}{x^2 - y^2} \]
Factoring numerator and denominator:
\[ = \frac{(x - y)^2}{(x - y)(x + y)} = \frac{x - y}{x + y} \]
(Alternatively: \( \frac{-x - (-y)}{-x + (-y)} = \frac{-(x - y)}{-(x + y)} = \frac{x - y}{x + y} \)).
Answer: \( \frac{x - y}{x + y} \).

c) Set Theory (Inclusion-Exclusion Principle):

Total students \( U = 120 \) (numbered 1 to 120).
- Physics \( (P) \): Even numbers (multiples of 2):
\[ n(P) = \left\lfloor \frac{120}{2} \right\rfloor = 60 \]
- Chemistry \( (C) \): Multiples of 5:
\[ n(C) = \left\lfloor \frac{120}{5} \right\rfloor = 24 \]
- Math \( (M) \): Multiples of 7:
\[ n(M) = \left\lfloor \frac{120}{7} \right\rfloor = 17 \]
Now intersections:
- Physics and Chemistry \( (P \cap C) \): Multiples of LCM(2, 5) = 10:
\[ n(P \cap C) = \left\lfloor \frac{120}{10} \right\rfloor = 12 \]
- Physics and Math \( (P \cap M) \): Multiples of LCM(2, 7) = 14:
\[ n(P \cap M) = \left\lfloor \frac{120}{14} \right\rfloor = 8 \]
- Chemistry and Math \( (C \cap M) \): Multiples of LCM(5, 7) = 35:
\[ n(C \cap M) = \left\lfloor \frac{120}{35} \right\rfloor = 3 \]
- All three subjects \( (P \cap C \cap M) \): Multiples of LCM(2, 5, 7) = 70:
\[ n(P \cap C \cap M) = \left\lfloor \frac{120}{70} \right\rfloor = 1 \]
By the Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion:
\[ n(P \cup C \cup M) = n(P) + n(C) + n(M) - [n(P \cap C) + n(P \cap M) + n(C \cap M)] + n(P \cap C \cap M) \]
\[ = 60 + 24 + 17 - (12 + 8 + 3) + 1 = 101 - 23 + 1 = 79 \]
Number of students who opted for none of the three subjects:
\[ n(\text{None}) = U - n(P \cup C \cup M) = 120 - 79 = 41 \]
Answer: 41 students.
